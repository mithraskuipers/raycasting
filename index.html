Raycasting

Five interactive stops covering the mathematics behind classic raycasting engines - from a single ray-wall intersection to a full corrected 3D projection you can walk around in. Every formula updates live as you step through and tweak the controls. Work through them in order - each stop leans on the algorithm from the one before it.

Everything at once
Walk Mode strings together every concept from the four tabs to its left and runs it live, every frame:

1. Cast. For each vertical strip of the screen, a ray P(t) = O + t·D is fired from the player at a slightly different angle across the field of view.

2. Walk the grid. Each ray steps through the map with the DDA algorithm, jumping from grid line to grid line until it lands on a wall cell.

3. Correct for fisheye. The raw ray length is projected back onto the view axis with cos(δ), so straight walls stay straight instead of bowing outward at the screen edges.

4. Project to height. Each strip is drawn with height = k / distance - closer walls fill more of the screen, farther walls shrink and darken. Strips also shade slightly differently depending on which axis the ray crossed to hit the wall (the same "side" flag from the DDA tab) - a cheap depth cue that makes adjoining walls read as distinct surfaces instead of one flat blob.

5. Move and collide. The arrow keys nudge the player's position along its facing direction and rotate that facing - plain vector math, same O and D from Vector Basics. Before a move is accepted, a small collision box around the player is checked against the map; if one axis would clip into a wall it's blocked while the other axis still slides through, so brushing along a wall doesn't feel like hitting a full stop.
